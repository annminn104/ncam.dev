# AGENTS.md — apps/cd-player

## What this is

**CD Player**: any Spotify playlist or album, played on a 3D CD player of the visitor's choice:
the suitcase (the default) in four colourways, Blush (the pink it opens in), Sage, Cream and
Cherry, or the Clear model, an upright, see-through player whose shell, frame, board and light
each take a colour of their own. The next five discs wait in a rack, in the suitcase's open lid or
on a stand beside the Clear, each printed with its first artist's photo, and when a track is about
to play the next disc flies from rack slot 0 onto the spindle, on the Clear through a round glass
door that opens for it. A crystal case shows the album art on its booklet (beside the suitcase's
rack, on an easel on the Clear's other side), a plate and a luggage tag on the handle name the
collection, and the page behind the player is a pastel of its accent (the skin's, or the Clear's
light's) with the album art blurred into it. The server renders that page with a loader where the
player will stand, never a picture of it, and the scene fades in over the loader once it is
drawn. Beside it, "Popular on Spotify" offers twelve of Spotify's own playlists a press away. A
self-contained Module Federation remote (`cd_player`, :9011) that the host mounts at
`/projects/cd-player`. The default collection is the playlist `37i9dQZEVXbMDoHDwVN2tF`,
"Top 50 - Global" (`DEFAULT_COLLECTION` in `src/routes.ts`).

The stack: React 19, React Three Fiber 9 and three 0.186 (in the lazy scene chunk only), zod (in
the server code only), `@ncam/logger`, and Manrope (the page's text) and Fraunces (the canvas
prints only) through `@fontsource`; the small labels and the numbers use the system's monospace.
Two models, one GLB each, generated by the Blender scripts in `art/`: `public/assets/cd-player.glb`
(the suitcase) and `public/assets/cd-player-clear.glb` (the Clear). The scene downloads the chosen
model's alone, so the default page never fetches the Clear's.

## What it exposes

`vite.config.ts` (`defineRemote`, which sets `shared: {}` and `dts: false`) exposes three entries:

- `./mount` (`src/mount.tsx`): `mount(target, config?)`, a client render. It injects
  `src/styles/app.css` as `<style id="cd-player-styles">` (removed on unmount) and imports the
  fonts (`src/fonts.ts`).
- `./ssr` (`src/ssr.tsx`): `renderHeroSSR({ config?, assetBase? })` returns `{ html, css, head? }`.
  `html` is the whole shell (link bar and its presets, tracklist, now-playing card, backdrop, the
  loader in the scene's place, help line) for the collection the server fetched, followed by
  `<script type="application/json" id="cdp-state">` holding that data (`src/ssr-state.ts`, escaped
  so it can never end the script). `css` is `src/styles/fonts.css`, its `url()`s rebased onto
  this remote's origin, plus `src/styles/app.css`. `head` (`src/head.ts`: the collection's name
  and a description with its owner or artist and its track count) comes only on `/playlist/<id>`
  and `/album/<id>` once the collection has loaded; on `/` the host keeps its own head.
  `assetBase` is accepted for the contract and ignored.
- `./hydrate` (`src/hydrate.tsx`): `hydrate(target, config?)` reads `#cdp-state` back, hydrates
  the server markup (the host inlined the CSS) and renders the state script again, so the trees
  match.

**Route-aware** (registry `routeAware: true`). Both client entries return a `MountHandle` with
`update(route)`, which sets the route in `src/route-controller.ts`, outside React (`App` reads it
with `useSyncExternalStore`): changing collections never remounts the remote, its WebGL context
or the Spotify embed. `navigate()` only asks, through `config.onNavigate`; the host calls
`update()` back. Standalone, `src/standalone.tsx` pushes the history entry itself and follows
`popstate`. The routes (`src/routes.ts`): `/`, `/playlist/<id>` and `/album/<id>` with a
22-character base62 id; anything else renders like `/`. In the host they sit under
`/projects/cd-player`.

`config` is the host's `MountConfig`. `config.assetBase`, else the bundle's own URL
(`remoteBase()` in `src/lib/asset-url.ts`), names the GLBs and the proxy, since the host page may
be on another origin.

## Data

`GET /api/spotify?type=<playlist|album|track|artist>&id=<22-character id>`
(`src/server/spotify-handler.ts`) answers with this remote's JSON (`src/spotify/types.ts`):

- `playlist` and `album`: `{ type, id, name, subtitle, tracks }`, a track being
  `{ id, uri, title, artists, durationMs, playable, explicit }`; `subtitle` is the owner or the
  artist, and only `spotify:track:` entries are kept (a podcast episode in a playlist is dropped);
- `track`: `{ id, art: [{ url, width }], palette, artists: [{ id, name }] }`, the album art
  smallest first and the palette Spotify's own background, tinted and text colours;
- `artist`: `{ id, name, images: [{ url, width }], palette }`.

Statuses: 200; 400 `{"error":"bad_request"}` for another type or a malformed id (`no-store`); 404
`{"error":"not_found"}` for an id Spotify has no page for (`public, max-age=60, s-maxage=300`);
502 `{"error":"upstream"}` for anything else Spotify does, a status, a stall past 6 s or a page
that does not parse (`no-store`); 405 for a method other than GET, HEAD and OPTIONS, and 204 for
OPTIONS. Collections are cached `public, max-age=300, s-maxage=3600, stale-while-revalidate=86400`
(Top 50 changes daily), tracks and artists `public, max-age=3600, s-maxage=86400,
stale-while-revalidate=604800`. The handler sends its own `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, and
`vercel.json` keeps its blanket header off `/api/`, so it is never doubled. A 502 is logged
(`cd-player.upstream-failed`, with the ref and the error, whose message names the stage or the
shape that failed) so a layout change on Spotify's side shows in the deployment's logs; the answer
stays opaque. The line has `@ncam/logger`'s format but is a plain `console.warn`: that package's
entry is TypeScript source, and the Function's graph keeps to relative `.js` imports (below).

One handler runs in three places:

- **The Vercel Function** `api/spotify.ts` (a default export with `fetch(request)`). The runtime
  imports of its graph end in `.js` (`spotify-handler.ts` imports `../routes.js` and
  `../spotify/embed.js`, which imports `./schema.js`): Node ESM in the Function resolves
  specifiers as written. Keep that for any runtime import added to those files, and add no
  workspace package to them; no test covers it, and only the deployed Function would fail.
- **Vite middleware** on the dev and preview servers (`spotifyProxy()` in `vite.config.ts`), at the
  same path. Vite's own CORS middleware (on, from `defineRemote`) runs first and answers a
  preflight itself; on every other request the handler's header replaces its Allow-Origin. Since
  the config loads that graph too, its relative imports, type imports included (`embed.ts`'s
  `../routes.js` and `./types.js`), and the config's own end in `.js`: Vite prints an advisory
  about its `configLoader: 'native'` on every start for each one that does not.
- **In-process for the server render.** `src/server/initial.ts` (`loadInitial`) calls
  `src/spotify/embed.ts` directly for the collection, its first playable track and that track's
  first artist (the backdrop and the case), one after another inside a **1.5 s budget** they
  share: each fetch gets what is left as its own timeout, and one with nothing left is skipped.
  It never rejects. A miss is logged once (`cd-player.ssr-data-failed`, with the stage) and the
  client fetches that part after hydrating. A complete answer is kept for ten minutes, at most 64
  of them, the oldest out first (the host's server process outlives its requests).

**Unofficial, and where it can break.** The embed pages (`open.spotify.com/embed/<type>/<id>`)
hold their data as JSON in `__NEXT_DATA__`, at `props.pageProps.state.data.entity`. That is
undocumented, so all of it stays in `src/spotify/embed.ts` (the fetch, with a desktop Chrome
`User-Agent` because some clients get a page without data, and `readEntity`) and
`src/spotify/schema.ts` (zod, only the fields used). A change on Spotify's side becomes a 502,
never a crash: a track entry that does not fit is skipped, but a non-empty list where none fits
fails as upstream. zod and the server code stay out of the client entries' graphs
(`src/ssr-graph.test.ts`).

The browser's client (`src/spotify/client.ts`) resolves `/api/spotify` against the remote's
origin, makes one request per item at a time, keeps answers for the page's life (failures are not
kept) and is seeded with what the server render fetched. A 404 reads "We couldn't open that
playlist. It may be private or deleted." (or "that album": `requestErrorMessage` names the type
asked for), anything else "Spotify didn't answer. Try again in a moment." The engine fetches the
details and artist of the current track and the next five (`PREFETCH` in
`src/playback/engine.ts`).

**Retry** (`canRetry` in `client.ts`: a 502, a stall the proxy turned into one, the network, a
body that would not read; never a 404 or a refused request). A pasted link or a preset that failed
that way shows its message under the input with a small **Retry** beside it, which loads the same
collection again (`src/link-loader.ts`, see "Personalisation"). A first load that failed (no
collection, nothing loading, an error: Spotify down on `/` or on a shared link) shows an **empty
shelf** in the tracklist's place, the message and, for those failures, Retry, which calls
`engine.open(ref)` again: `open` asks again for a ref whose collection never came. The bar's
title then says "No tracks" rather than "Loading…", the message is not repeated under the input,
and on a phone the sheet opens by itself, once per failure, to show the shelf (`App` sets
`tracksOpen` when a new error appears, so the Tracks toggle can close the sheet and open it again;
a Retry that fails again opens it again). The engine's snapshot keeps the failure as
`error: { message, retry }`. A failed change of collection
keeps the one shown, with its message in the bar and no Retry (`open` does not ask again while a
collection is shown); a pasted link is fetched before the page goes to it, so that case is rare.

**Pictures.** Spotify's image CDN answers with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, so the scene loads
the art and the artist photos straight into textures with `crossOrigin = 'anonymous'`
(`src/scene/textures/images.ts`, an LRU of 24 images). There is no image proxy.

## Playback

**The embed loads on the first play action only** (`src/playback/embed-controller.ts`,
`IFRAME_API_SRC` = `https://open.spotify.com/embed/iframe-api/v1`), never with the page: that
click is the user activation the browser's autoplay policy wants, and it keeps the embed's
third-party cookies out of the first load, which Lighthouse's Best Practices audit counts. The
engine (`PlayerEngine.user()` in `src/playback/engine.ts`) builds the player only when a press
would start playback (the sequencer would emit a `swap` or a `load`): Repeat, Shuffle, Stop or a
seek before that loads nothing. `App`'s `createPlayer` hands it a fresh `<div>` inside the
now-playing card's `.cdp-now__embed`, where React renders nothing; Spotify swaps the div for its
iframe, 80 px high and the card's width. The script gets 15 s, building the player 20 s
(`PLAYER_TIMEOUT_MS`). If either fails (an ad blocker, a privacy setting), the card says "The
Spotify player could not load here…", the sequencer goes back to idle keeping the track, the
order, Repeat and Shuffle, and the visitor's next press tries again.

**The first load only plays.** `createController(el, { uri })` already loads the track the player
is built on, and Spotify's script never takes a uri for the one it holds (its `currentUri` is never
set), so a `loadUri` of the same track would load the embed's page a second time. The first
`load()` of that uri in `createEmbedPlayer` therefore calls `play()` alone, which the script holds
until its iframe is ready. Every other load is `loadUri` then `play`, a later load of that same
track too: once a track has ended, the embed has dropped its list. Spotify's `'error'` events (a
uri its script refuses, a playback error its iframe posts) are logged as `cd-player.embed-error`
with whatever they carry; a command the iframe cannot carry out ("no list was loaded") throws inside
it and never arrives as one.

**The sequencer and its commands** (`src/playback/sequencer.ts`): a pure reducer,
`step(state, event)` returns the next state and the commands for the world outside: `swap {to}`
(change the disc in the scene), `load {uri}`, `pause`, `resume` and `seek {ms}` (the embed). The
status is `idle`, `swapping`, `playing` or `paused`. A track change emits `swap`; the engine runs it
through the bridge (`src/bridge.ts`, `swap(trackView)`) and sends **`SWAP_DONE`** once the scene
reports the disc seated, and only then does the sequencer emit **`load`**, so the music starts as
the tonearm lands. Without a running scene (before it boots, no WebGL) the bridge has no swapper
and the change is instant. Only the latest change may answer (a swap token), and a change that
fails still counts as seated.

The rules: Next and Previous skip unplayable tracks (disabled in the list); Previous more than 3 s
into a track (`RESTART_MS`) restarts it; Repeat wraps the list at both ends; Shuffle is a seeded
Fisher–Yates order with the current track moved to the front; Stop pauses and goes back to the
start; a seek is held between 0 and a second before the end; a new collection keeps Repeat and
drops Shuffle. The embed ignores a seek to under a second, so `seek` in `createEmbedPlayer` takes
the start its own way: a playing track restarts (the controller's `restart()`), a paused one waits
at its first second, since a restart would play it.

Keyboard (`App.tsx`): Space plays or pauses, N and P change tracks, never with a modifier or while
focus is in a field or on a button (so Space on a button, or on the slider, is the browser's own),
and a held key is one press. Every music action from the DOM also starts the scene while it waits.

**The card's controls** (`src/shell/NowPlaying.tsx`) give every 3D control a DOM equivalent, for the
keyboard, a screen reader and a phone. The head has Previous, Play or Pause and Next (32 px square
keys, `cdp-key`; Play `cdp-key--main`); the row under it has the **Seek** slider
(`src/shell/SeekSlider.tsx`, the knob's counterpart) and the small **Stop**, **Repeat** and
**Shuffle** keys (28 px, `cdp-key--small`). DOM order is the Tab order: Previous, Play, Next, Seek,
Stop, Repeat, Shuffle (Tab skips the slider while it is disabled). Repeat and Shuffle are switches:
`aria-pressed`, and when on an accent tint, border and icon and a dot under it, so the state is not
colour alone. Shuffle sends `TOGGLE_SHUFFLE` with `Date.now()` as the seed, as the deck's MODE key
does (`onAction` in `engine.ts`). All of them go through `act`, so a press while the scene waits
also starts it.

The slider is a native `<input type="range">` counted in whole seconds (`seekRange` in `src/view.ts`
floors, as `formatDuration` does), disabled while the status is `idle` or `swapping` (a seek does
nothing while the disc changes) or the duration is 0, with an `aria-valuetext` like "1:23 of
3:45". **One seek per gesture**: React's `onChange` is the `input` event, one per step of a drag,
and only sets a held value that the thumb shows, so the position
updates that keep arriving cannot pull it from under the finger. The native `change` event (bound
through a ref, since React does not hand it out) sends `SEEK` once, when the visitor lets go or at
each arrow-key press, and ends the hold. A gesture that ends with no `change` (Chrome sends none
for a cancelled touch) ends it on its own, a task after the pointer is released or cancelled. A
seek under a second goes through `restart()` (above), so a drag or Home to 0 needs nothing here.
It is drawn as a 2 px track, filled in the accent up to the position, in a 28 px tall hit area, with
a 10 px square thumb that is laid out and drawn only while it shows: on hover, keyboard focus and
press, and always where there is no hover (`@media (hover: none)`). `--cdp-shown` in
`src/styles/app.css` is 1 then, so at rest the track fills exactly to the position, and with the
thumb out the fill ends under its centre (the end moves a few px as the thumb comes in). The
`::-webkit-slider-*` rules have been looked at in Chromium; the `::-moz-range-*` ones in no browser
yet.

**The card's height comes out of the stage on a phone.** The card has a grid row of its own there
and the stage, which the canvas fills, takes what is left (see "The look"), so a taller card never
covers the player: it leaves it a smaller canvas, as the embed's 80 px do once, at the first play.
The row is 8 + 28 + 6 px (its margins and the 28 px slider and keys); a note that stands where the
embed would be (`cdp-now__note--lead`) follows it with no top margin of its own, the row's 6 px
being its gap.

**When a track has ended** (`hasEnded`): an update paused within 500 ms of the end, or snapped back
to 0 just after being within 1.5 s of it (the same uri), never while buffering. ENDED then moves
on, or goes idle at the end of the list without Repeat. After a `load`, an event that still names
the track just left is dropped (a late "paused at its end" would end the new one); any other uri
passes, because Spotify may report a relinked track under another id.

**The end watchdog** (`watchEnd` in `engine.ts`). Logged out, the embed often lets a preview run
out without a word: no paused update, no end. The sequencer would stay `playing` in silence while
the embed has dropped its list, and any command after that (a pause, a seek, a restart, the
collection load's pause) throws "Cannot perform operation; no list was loaded." inside its iframe.
So every update of a track that plays (neither paused nor buffering) re-arms one timer for the rest
of the track plus 1.5 s (`END_GRACE_MS`); a paused update (an end the embed reports among them), a
load, a new collection and dispose clear it, and a timer that fires after a newer update does
nothing. If it runs out with the status `playing` or `paused`, the engine sends ENDED itself: the
disc changes without a `pause` (the sequencer's ENDED path sends none), and the next `load` gives
the embed a new list. `paused` counts because a Pause or Stop pressed after the embed went quiet
sets it with no help from the embed, and its `pause` throws inside the dead iframe, so no paused
update comes back to clear the watch: a live embed answers a pause with one, so a watch still valid
while `paused` means no answer came. `hasEnded` still asks for a paused update, since a `restart()`
near the end also snaps to 0. A buffering update neither arms nor clears it, so an embed that keeps
reporting buffering at the end does not hold it off; a stall longer than the rest of the track and
1.5 s moves on.

**The hint.** Right after `loadUri` the embed reports itself paused until the audio starts, so the
engine ignores paused updates until `playback_started`. If that has not come 3.5 s after a load
(`HINT_MS`), the card says "Press play inside the Spotify card to start." (an iframe the browser
would not let play by itself), and the note goes once playback starts. A slow machine can take
longer than that over the first track (7 to 11 s from the press in headless Chromium on
SwiftShader), so the hint may show for a moment and go by itself.

**No volume.** The iFrame API has no volume, so the knob scrubs (see "The scene"), and the card's
Seek slider does the same in the DOM. Per Spotify's Widget Terms the widget stays visible in the
card while it plays: never hide it, and never play the audio any other way.

## The scene

**A lazy chunk.** `src/shell/SceneHost.tsx` imports `src/scene/PlayerScene.tsx` behind
`import.meta.env.SSR`, so the server build drops it, and only once the boot starts. Nothing `App`
imports reaches three, `@react-three/*` or `src/scene/` statically; `src/ssr-graph.test.ts` walks
the three entries' graphs and pins it. The files the scene shares with the shell (`src/bridge.ts`,
`actions.ts`, `scene-props.ts`, `models.ts`, `looks.ts`, `skins.ts`, `clear-looks.ts`, `view.ts`,
`playback/sequencer.ts`, `lib/asset-url.ts`, `fonts.ts`) stay three-free for that reason, and so
do `boot.ts`, `failure.ts`, `webgl.ts` and `autoplay.ts`, which ask about WebGL without three.

**The phases** (`src/boot.ts`, a pure reducer, garden-home's shape): `waiting` → `booting` →
`live`, or `error` over the backdrop. Events: `start` ("Open the player", and "Try again" for a
failure that can be retried in place), `autostart`, `ready` (three frames of the player drawn,
`useReadyProbe` in `PlayerRig.tsx`) and `fail`. `autostart` only ever leaves `waiting`: it comes
when the page has settled on a hardware GPU and with every music action from the DOM, so a failed
boot is retried by "Try again" alone. While booting the scene layer is invisible and `inert`, under
the loader; on `ready` it fades in over 600 ms and the loader fades out with it (`FADE_MS` in
`src/App.tsx` equals the `.cdp-scene` and `.cdp-loader` transitions in `src/styles/app.css`, which
`src/styles/app-css.test.ts` holds), then the loader unmounts (see "First load").

**Autostart on a hardware GPU** (`src/autoplay.ts`, the same logic as mini-store's and
garden-home's copies; keep the three in step): once the page has loaded and the main thread is
idle (`whenSettled`), the scene starts by itself when WebGL2 runs on a hardware GPU (a context made
with `failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat`, and a renderer that is not SwiftShader, llvmpipe, softpipe or
Microsoft Basic Render) and the visitor has not asked for reduced motion. On a software rasteriser
(the Lighthouse runner) the page waits for the visitor: the loader gives way to "Open the player".

**Failures** (`src/failure.ts`, shown by `src/shell/EnterButton.tsx` in a `role="alert"` where the
loader stood, over the backdrop; the music plays on): no WebGL2 (`hasWebGL2()` in `src/webgl.ts`,
asked before any canvas mounts): "This browser can't draw the 3D player. The music still plays.",
with no button; a chunk that would not load (`SceneChunkError`): "The 3D player couldn't start. The
music still plays." with **Reload the page** (a browser remembers a failed dynamic import, and the
reload stops the music, hence the label); anything else (the boot's GLB, the model contract, a
render-time throw caught by `SceneBoundary`, a boot that stalls on the scene's code or on the GLB's
download: `BootStallError`, see "Hand-overs"): the same message with **Try again**. Each is logged
as `cd-player.scene-failed`. A model chosen once another is on show that will not load fails
nothing: the model on show stays (see "Changing models").

**The GLB contract** (`src/scene/contract.ts`), one for both models: `REQUIRED_NODES` are the
pivots `lid_hinge`, `arm_pivot`, `knob` and `btn_prev`, `btn_next`, `btn_play`, `btn_stop`,

(the luggage tag's printed face), the empties `disc_seat` (on the spindle), `case_seat` and
`rack_slot_0` … `rack_slot_4` (under `lid_hinge`, each the pose of a disc in the rack: slot 0 is
the lowest, nearest the player), and the VU strip's `vu_seg_0` … `vu_seg_9`. `lid_hinge` is the
suitcase's lid and the Clear's stand (the page moves neither). `OPTIONAL_NODES` are the nodes a
model may have, the Clear's door `door_hinge`: the loader takes each one it finds, and the rig
drives it where the model's profile has a door (below). `DYNAMIC_MATERIALS` are the five
materials the page paints (`art_disc`, `art_booklet`, `card_print`, `tag_print`, `lcd_screen`).
`loadPlayerModel(profile, url, …)` (`src/scene/model.ts`, GLTFLoader with the meshopt decoder)
throws `ModelContractError` naming what is missing. `src/scene/model-contract.test.ts` reads both
committed GLBs. The suitcase's: every required node, every printed material and a role for every
material, a material for every skin role, UVs on every printed and tiled primitive, the rack slots
as empties under the lid, the tag's and the VU segments' materials, the deck's printed marks off
the keys and the knob, the tonearm's moving parts under `arm_pivot` and its rest on the deck, none

(`CLEAR_ROLES`), the rack slots as empties under `lid_hinge`, the tag's and the VU segments'
materials, the budget, and meshes on leaf nodes only.

**The scene profiles** (`src/scene/profiles.ts`, `SCENE_PROFILES`, three-free): one
`SceneProfile` per model, what the scene needs of it beyond its name and GLB (`src/models.ts`).
The frame `{ target, direction, across, up }` is where the camera aims, from which way, and the
half-width and half-height it fits round its aim (see "The one framing rule"); `near` is an
optional near plane; the arm `{ rest, play }` gives the arm's angles about its pivot's Y, at rest
and over the disc; the door `{ angle } | null` how far the door swings open about its hinge's Y
(null: no door); the flight `{ reach: { slot, spindle }, aside }` a disc's way to and from the
spindle (see "The rig"). `profiles.test.ts` keeps the suitcase's as it is framed and driven, and
pins the Clear's door, arm and flights.

**Material roles** (`src/scene/material-roles.ts`, three-free so the contract test can read it;
`materials.ts` builds them). The GLB carries the base colour, roughness and metalness tuned in
Blender; the role adds what glTF cannot say: leather (sheen, a light clearcoat and a pebble-grain
normal map made at runtime; `trim`, the piping, handle, tag and rack frame, is leather too),
fabric (a plain-weave normal map, for the speaker cloth and the lid's `strap`, the Clear's tag
cord too), lining (a diamond-quilt normal map, 25 mm diamonds over the 5 cm box UVs), plastic,
matte, crystal (transmission 1, IOR 1.53, a faint specular so the booklet reads through it), metal
(`hardware`, which is every metal fitting; `chrome`; the plate's `alu_side`), brushed
(anisotropy), disc-data and foil (iridescence), gloss-black, emissive (`led`, `lcd`, and `vu`,
which the page clones per segment), print and screen. The Clear adds three: clear-shell
(`clear_shell`: transmission 1, IOR 1.49, roughness 0.05, its front faces only, so the inside
shows through one wall at a time), frame (`clear_frame`, with the GLB's roughness and metalness
until a look sets them) and pcb (`board`, a glossy solder mask under a 0.6 clearcoat); its other
materials take the roles above (`door_glass` crystal, `light` emissive, `chip` gloss-black,
`copper`, `solder` and `magnet` metal, among them). The three maps are `leatherNormal`,
`weaveNormal` and `quiltNormal` in `textures/grain.ts`, tileable height fields turned into
normals; `SKIN_ROLES` maps the suitcase's seven recolourable materials (`leather`, `trim`,
`thread`, `lining`, `hardware`, `deck`, `grille`) to the skins' roles, and `CLEAR_ROLES` the
Clear's four (`clear_shell`, `clear_frame`, `board`, `light`) to its parts. A name without a role
falls back to plastic, but the contract test fails first. A look then recolours the materials
those two name, in place (see "Looks"). The studio is in `PlayerScene.tsx`: a PMREM'd
`RoomEnvironment` (`scene.environmentIntensity` sets
every material's reflections), a key light that casts (2048 px shadow map, 1024 on a narrow
screen), a rim and a hemisphere light, AgX tone mapping, a device pixel ratio of at most 2 (1.5 on
a narrow screen), a transparent canvas and a shadow-only floor, so the page's backdrop shows round
the player.

**The rig** (`src/scene/rig.ts`, `rigFor`, one per model, from its profile): **seven disc copies**
(one on the spindle, five in the rack, one leaving), each with its own `art_disc` material so each
prints its own track, and **one crystal case**, the model's own with an `art_booklet` material of
its own. The disc copies live at the root and move in world space between the spindle
(`disc_seat`) and the rack's slots (`rack_slot_0` … `rack_slot_4`, empties under `lid_hinge`, each
the pose of a disc in it; slot `i` holds `view.upcoming[i]`). Slot 0 is the lowest and the
frontmost, nearest the player; each slot above it stands higher and further back (in the
suitcase's lid 20 mm higher, 1.6 mm nearer the lining and leaning out 1.5° less; on the Clear's
stand 20 mm higher and 18 mm further back, every disc leaning back 15°), so each disc stands behind
the one below it, and the queue advancing moves each disc a slot down. The ways follow from that:
a disc flying to or from the spindle leaves one place and reaches the other along each one's
normal, a slot's or the spindle seat's own (`seat.out`: straight up out of the suitcase's deck, out
through the Clear's door), on a cubic Bézier whose handles reach the profile's `flight.reach`
(`rig.flight`, `reachOf`): 8 cm out of the suitcase's lid, past its rim, and out of its deck;
30 cm out of the Clear's stand, whose slots stand 3 to 10 cm behind the shell's front, so a disc
that turned in sooner would cut through the shell's side, and 10 cm out through its door. It takes
off from a slot only when nothing stands in front of it; between slots a disc moves in a straight
line (with neighbours moving 60 ms apart the gaps only widen, as between discs further apart,
where a pop-out would cross them); a rack disc leaves and arrives by shrinking or growing where it
stands (any way out of the rack would pass through the discs in front); a disc leaving the spindle
goes out of sight at the profile's `flight.aside` from its seat, shrinking away (`asideOf`: up and
to the left of the suitcase's, out of the Clear's door and up to its right, away from the door's
hinge), and one from the side comes in the same way. `rig.test.ts` flies every way to and from the
Clear's spindle against its shell's walls: each one passes through the door's opening. The case
stays at `case_seat` and only turns over for a new booklet: it rises out of its seat by the reach
of its meshes across the turn (half its width and its depth) and a centimetre (measured at load,
so it never cuts into the lid or the Clear's easel), turns over about its own vertical axis and
back, and settles, and it turns only while no disc flies to or from the spindle. The booklet has
two prints, the one that shows and the next turn's (`bookletFront`), swapped as the case passes
edge-on. The arm turns by the director's angles (`ARM_REST` to `ARM_PLAY`), mapped onto the
profile's own
(`modelArmAngle`: the suitcase's 0 to −0.52 rad about its pivot's Y, the Clear's 0 to +0.52, its
pivot's Y being the disc's normal); the door (`rig.door`: the hinge, its shut angle and the
profile's turn, −100° on the Clear, out toward the viewer) opens as far as the pose's `door`
says, 0 shut to 1 open. What the stage holds is plain data the rig carries (`src/scene/stage.ts`,
three-free): the `Shelf` (the copy on the spindle and in each slot, the track each carries, the
booklet's track, the collection) and where the last pose left the arm.

**How a change reads** (`classifyChange` in `stage.ts`). The bridge hands the scene only
`swap(to)`, and the engine publishes the view the change leads to before it asks, so the scene
compares `to` and that view with what its shelf holds: the track in the player is the new
`upcoming[0]` → **prev**; `to` is in slot 0 → **first** (nothing on the spindle) or **next** (a
natural end too); `to` is in slot `k` ≥ 1 → **pick**; anything else, an empty rack included →
**side**; a collection whose name or subtitle differs → **collection**. Tracks match by id: of two
slots holding a track a playlist has twice, the one whose next slot holds the new `upcoming[0]` is
the one played, else the first; a two-track list on Repeat reads Next as prev. A view that changes
with no swap (a new collection, Shuffle, Repeat) is followed each frame between changes, as
**collection** or **restack**. Whatever the reading, every change ends with the shelf exactly as
the view says: a slot whose incoming disc does not hold its track gets a new disc.

**The director's timelines** (`src/scene/director.ts`: a plan of moves per change, and pure
functions of the time, tested). The arm lifts and swings to its rest over the first 400 ms and
swings back in and lowers over 2400–2800 ms of a 2.8 s change, the spin following it.

- _first_ (1.6 s): slot 0's disc flies down onto the spindle (100–800 ms), the discs behind it each
  move a slot nearer (from 450 ms, slot 1's first, 60 ms apart), a new disc grows into slot 4
  (1000–1350), the arm swings in and lowers (1150–1500);
- _next_ (2.8 s, a track's natural end too): the spindle's disc rises and flies aside (450–1050),
  slot 0's disc flies down (900–1600), the rack advances (from 1250) and a new disc grows into
  slot 4 (1850–2200), the case turns for the new booklet once the disc has landed (1600–2500);
- _prev_ (2.8 s): the case turns first (300–1200); slot 4's disc shrinks away (400–700) and the
  rack moves back a slot (from 700, the top disc first), the spindle's disc goes back into slot 0
  (1200–1900), the previous track's disc comes in from the side onto the spindle (1700–2350);
- _pick_ (2.8 s): the discs in front of the picked one shrink away (from 300 ms, 90 ms apart) and
  the spindle's leaves, the picked one flies down (900–1600), the ones behind it close the gap
  (from 1250) and new ones grow in above them (from 1700, 90 ms apart), the case turns
  (1600–2500);
- _side_ (2.8 s, also a swap into another collection): the spindle's disc leaves, the track comes
  in from the side (1100–1800), the slots whose track changes reprint as a cascade (shrinking from
  450 ms and growing from 1500 ms, 90 ms apart), the case turns (1800–2700);
- _cascade_ (1.6 s, a view that changed with no swap): the changed slots shrink from 0 ms and new
  discs grow in from 800 ms, 90 ms apart; the spindle's disc leaves (450–1050, the arm lifting off
  first) when nothing is in the player any more, and the case turns after it (1050–1950);
  otherwise the arm holds its pose (Shuffle while a track plays never lifts it).

The case's turn takes 900 ms: it rises for 200, turns over for 250, back for 250 and settles for
200, and the booklet's print swaps as it passes edge-on, 325 ms in (`FLIP_EDGE_MS`). An arriving
disc reuses a copy that went out of sight at least 200 ms before (`PRINT_LEAD_MS`), a spare one
first. `director.test.ts` walks every timeline for these rules: nothing in front of a disc taking
off, a slot empty before a disc comes into it, no disc flying while the case turns. `restingPose`
holds between changes: every copy where the shelf puts it, the arm on the disc while a track is
loaded, 200 rpm while it plays, the door shut.

**The door** (the Clear's; the swap controller's `door` option, set for a rig with one, has
`planChange` pass each plan through `withDoor`). The door is all the way open while a disc leaves
the spindle or comes onto it: it swings open over the 250 ms before the first such move
(`DOOR_OPEN_MS`) and shuts over 300 ms once the last of them is over (`DOOR_SHUT_MS`); a plan with
no move at the spindle keeps it shut (`door` null). A plan whose first such move starts sooner
than the door can open waits for it, everything in it that much later (`delayPlan`, the arm's
swing too, `ArmPlan.at`): only _first_ does, its disc flying at 100 ms, so a first play on the Clear
takes 150 ms longer. `doorAt` is the door's opening at a time, eased; a change starts and ends with
it shut, so one under reduced motion, which jumps to its end, ends shut. `director.test.ts` walks
every timeline with the door: all the way open while a disc leaves or reaches the spindle, shut at
both ends, a plan that waits for it, and nothing changed for a model without one.

`src/scene/swap-controller.ts` is the bridge's swapper, with no React and an injected clock so node
tests drive it, and every change it runs settles: a new change first ends the one in progress, a
change waits at most 1.5 s (`PRINT_WAIT_MS`) for its first prints and for the pictures of those it
prints on the way (`preload`), a change asked for in a hidden page finishes at once, a running one
also ends on the clock (its duration + 250 ms, a door's wait and swing included) when no frame
comes, and a teardown settles the one in progress at once. A change gives out its later prints as
its timeline reaches them (a reused copy once it is out of sight, a disc moving into slot 0 again
at 1024 px as it moves), and all of them at once if it ends early. `seat(view)` fills the shelf at
once as the scene boots and as each new model's rig comes on show (the track in the player on the
spindle, mid-playback included; the rack; the booklet) and prints it;
`follow(view)` runs each frame. `src/scene/useDirector.ts` draws the poses in R3F's frame loop and,
on the dev server only, puts `window.__cdpStage()` on the page: the shelf, what each copy is
printed with and at what size, and the change in progress.

**The VU strip** (`src/scene/vu.ts`, `vu-materials.ts`, `useVu.ts`): the ten segments share one
emissive `vu` material in the GLB and get a clone each at load (`vuMaterialsFor`, once per model,
disposed with it). `vuLevels(timeMs, playing, reducedMotion)` is pure: a smooth signal of the
play time (three sines under a slow swell) lights a prefix of the strip, with the highest segment
of the last 400 ms held at half; `playing` false is every segment off. The hook keeps its own
clock (`vuFrame`, pure too), which only advances while the status is `playing`, so the strip holds
still while paused and goes dark when idle or while a disc changes. The clock starts empty, so a
scene that boots on a paused track keeps the strip dark until a track plays. Under reduced motion
it uses half the strip, slowly. Its colours are the page's, not the GLB's, drawn outside the tone
mapping (the `emissive` role): the suitcase's amber, orange and red (`VU_COLOURS`), or the Clear's
light in ten steps brightening from 45 % of its glow to the glow itself (`vuColours(look)`, which
`PlayerRig` hands `useVu`). The audio stays inside Spotify's iframe: the strip is paced, never
measured.

**Printed textures** (`src/scene/textures/`): canvases, painted in the page's own faces
(`loadFaces` loads them first, because canvas text never asks the page for a font): each disc
copy's label (the artist's photo, else the album art, with the title and artists round the rim in
capitals: 1024 px on the spindle and in slot 0, 512 px in slots 1–4; a new size means a new
texture), the booklet's two prints (1024 px square, the album art; `textures/useDiscPrints.ts`
prints the discs and the booklet through its React-free core, `textures/disc-printer.ts`), the
plate (1024 × 182, "NOW SPINNING" and the collection as dark engraving on brushed steel,
`paintCard`), the luggage tag (512 × 768, the collection's name wrapped over up to three ruled
lines, `paintTag` with `wrapLines`) and the LCD (256 × 112, the track and the time in the
suitcase's cyan, `LCD_ON`, or the Clear's light, `lcdColour(look)`, repainted at most once a second
and whenever its colour changes). Before any art they show the page's own defaults.
`textures/useSceneTextures.ts` sets the plate, the tag and the LCD up once per rig, and the
display's colour is part of what its frame compares, so a new light reprints the display alone:
keep the colour out of the set-up's dependencies, or a light change puts the plate and the tag
back to their defaults until the view next changes. `canvasTexture` keeps **`flipY = false`**:
`export.py` lays each print's UVs over its mesh's local bounds in Blender and the glTF exporter
flips V, so the canvas's top row is the print's top, as GLTFLoader's own maps assume; with three's
default `flipY` a print would come out flipped top to bottom. The last print started on a disc
wins (`painter.ts`; the director aborts the older one); a copy printed for another track shows
that track's name round the default label at once and its art once loaded, so no copy ever shows a
track it no longer carries. A print that fails or stalls never stops a change, a print made in the
fallback faces is made again once the fonts are in, and `reprintFor` prints a track again when its
art arrives after its change began.
The engine fetches the art of the current track and the next five (`PREFETCH` in `engine.ts`), so
every slot prints its track's art once it arrives.

**Controls and the orbit** (`src/scene/controls.ts`, `useControls.ts`, `interaction.ts`,
`orbit.ts`, `CameraRig.tsx`):

- **The deck keys** press on the **primary button** (or a touch), and only the nearest mesh under
  the pointer counts. On pointerdown `btn_prev`, `btn_next`, `btn_play` (play or pause), `btn_stop`,
  `btn_rpt` (Repeat) and `btn_mode` (Shuffle) emit their `PlayerAction` through the bridge
  (`BUTTON_ACTIONS`), and the key sinks 1.2 mm and comes back over 160 ms (`PRESS_MS`). The cursor
  turns to a hand over a key or the knob. Every key has a DOM equivalent in the now-playing card
  (see "Playback").
- **The knob scrubs the track.** A turn is followed on the window, read around the middle of the
  knob's top (its pivot sits at its base), and one full turn is 30 s (`KNOB_MS_PER_TURN`). It emits
  one `seek {deltaMs}` only on a real release; a cancelled turn, or a mouse that moves with no
  button down (a native menu took the release), puts the knob back where the turn began and seeks
  nothing. The card's Seek slider is its DOM equivalent.
- **A drag elsewhere orbits the view** (the primary button or a touch), within ±25° of yaw and ±10°
  of pitch, eased (`orbit.ts`). It **recentres on a mouse double-click, or a touch double tap**
  (two taps within 350 ms and 30 px, neither moving more than 10 px), unless that lands on a
  control. `interaction.controlActive` tells the camera that the latest pointerdown went to a key
  or the knob (R3F's handler runs after the camera's), so that press neither orbits nor recentres;
  a mouse release that was lost (`releaseLost`) disarms both drags.
- The suitcase's printed marks (each key's dark ring `player_btn_gap_*` and label
  `player_btn_lbl_*`, the dial's ticks `player_tick_*` and its `player_vol`) are the deck's
  children in the model, so only the key or the knob itself moves and the page has no mark to
  re-parent; `model-contract.test.ts` holds that. The Clear's icons are printed on its keys' caps
  and go down with them.
- `.cdp-canvas` sets `touch-action: none` and `user-select: none`.

**The one framing rule** (`src/scene/framing.ts`):
`frameForViewport(width, height, yaw, pitch, model)` and `isWide(width)` (above 820 px the scene
draws as on a desktop; the layout's own breakpoints are the stylesheet's). The canvas fills the
stage alone (`.cdp-stage`, see "The look"), which no panel covers, so the frame aims at the
profile's `target` from its `direction` and stands as far back (`frameDistance`) as a box of the
profile's half-width `across` and half-height `up` round that aim asks for the canvas's shape: the
player sits in the middle of whatever the layout leaves it. The suitcase's two are equal, 0.285 m,
a sphere; the Clear's are 0.362 and 0.284 m, for its wide, low set, so a wide canvas fills with
it. `CameraRig` holds that frame for the model on show every frame (resizes included, a phone's
stage shrinking for the embed among them), with that model's near plane, and `PlayerScene` sizes
the shadow map and the pixel ratio by `isWide` of the window's width. `framing.test.ts` projects
each model's box through it (the Clear's is the whole set at rest with a disc in each slot, with
the door's sweep from shut to open as a second box) for canvas shapes from 0.2 to 7 wide for their
height, past every stage the layouts make (a phone's 220 px floor reaches about 3.6, a tall
window's rail about 0.26), swept in steps from 0.43 to 2.63: at the orbit's middle and its corners
the box stays 1 % of the canvas clear of every edge, and at the orbit's middle it stands within 3 %
of the canvas's middle and spans a floor or more of a side, 70 % for the suitcase and 60 % for the
Clear. Under the shared orbit, perspective swings the near end of the Clear's wide, low set toward
the camera on a wide canvas, so its height on screen grows about 1.6 times from the orbit's middle
to its ends, where 70 % at the middle and 1 % clear at the ends allow only 1.4. The Clear's boxes,
and the poses of its set that `rig.test.ts` flies through, were measured with three's GLTFLoader
over its committed GLB: measure them again whenever it changes. The near plane (`nearFor(model)`)
stands at 0.5 m for the suitcase (`NEAR`), as far out as the frame allows (the camera is never
nearer than about 0.8 m to the player's nearest corner), and at 0.52 m for the Clear (its
profile's `near`, a little more depth for the disc's printed layers than the suitcase needs); the
far one stands at 10 m (`FAR`). The disc's printed layers are microns apart, and a near plane at
0.02 m left the depth buffer too coarse to tell them apart (see "Known facts"). The test holds
each player between the two planes with 0.2 m to spare, and the foil six depth steps or more over
the label on every canvas half as wide as tall or wider.

**Changing models** (`src/scene/PlayerScene.tsx`, `src/scene/model-holder.ts`). The scene loads
the model the look names (`modelById(look.model)`, its `glb`), one load per wanted model, and
never empties the stage: the model on show stays drawn until the next one can take its place. A
loaded model is the next; `Precompile` compiles its shaders for the scene's lights and environment
(`gl.compileAsync`) before it is shown, so its first frames never stall on them, and it then takes
the stage with a rig of its own (`PlayerRig`, keyed by the model), seated from the view at once,
mid-playback included: the music never stops. The model it replaced goes once the new rig's ready
probe has seen its frames drawn, never before: the canvas commits after this component, and until
then a frame may still draw the old one. What the scene holds is `stepHeld`'s pure decision (the
model on show and the next, after a load lands, the look changes or a compile lands), which the
React-free holder (`createModelHolder`) applies, disposing every model it lets go of: a next that
a newer load replaces, or that the look stopped naming, is disposed and never shown, so a quick
Suitcase → Clear → Suitcase never draws the Clear, and `release` disposes everything as the scene
goes. `holder.want` runs in a layout effect, so the holder drops a next the look no longer names
within the commit that changes it: `Precompile`'s `onDone` carries the canvas's own last look,
which commits later, and a compile landing in between must find no next to swap in. The look goes
to the model it names alone, the one on show or the next, so the next swaps in already in its own
colours, while the model on show, its VU and its LCD keep the last look that named it (`worn`).
The boot's download reports through `onProgress` (the loader and the stall watch); every model
that comes on show, the boot's included, reports `ready` through `onModel` (`PlayerSceneProps`,
`ModelLoad` in `src/scene-props.ts`), and a later change also reports its download (`loading`,
which `App` keeps once per whole percent, `sameLoadPercent` in `src/view.ts`, as it keeps the
boot's) and, when the model never comes, `failed`: the model on show stays, the failure is logged
as `cd-player.model-failed` (with the model and the error), and `App` puts the look back on the
model shown. A change given up before it loads (the model on show chosen again) reports nothing
more, and its download is aborted, as is any load the scene lets go of.

**Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`): no autostart; a disc change and a cascade
in the rack are instant (the rig jumps to its end, the Clear's door shut, and the music still
waits a 300 ms beat, `REDUCED_MOTION_MS`); the backdrop's cross-fade, the tracklist's equaliser,
the loader's spin, the canvas's fade-in (and the loader's fade-out) and the phone sheet's slide
are off (`src/styles/app.css`). The disc still spins while a track plays, and the orbit still
eases.

## Personalisation

The URL names the collection: `/playlist/<id>` or `/album/<id>` (`src/routes.ts`), that is
`/projects/cd-player/playlist/<id>` in the host, so a page can be shared; **Share** in the link bar
copies `location.href` and says "Copied" on its button for two seconds, or "Couldn't copy" when the
clipboard refuses (a denied permission) or there is none (a page on plain http): `copyText` in
`src/shell/LinkBar.tsx` never rejects. Loading a pasted link (`loadLink` in `src/App.tsx`), or a
preset (see "Popular on Spotify"), which skips the first step:

1. `parseSpotifyLink` (`src/spotify/link.ts`) takes an `open.spotify.com` or `play.spotify.com` link
   with or without `https://`, `intl-xx/`, `embed/` or a query string, a `spotify:` URI, or a bare
   22-character id (taken as a playlist). A track, artist, episode, show or user link is refused as
   not a playlist or an album; `spotify.link` short links are not followed.
2. The link loader (`createLinkLoader` in `src/link-loader.ts`, three-free, tested on fakes)
   fetches the collection; only once that succeeds is `{ type, id }` saved in `localStorage` under
   **`ncam.cd-player.last`** (`src/persist.ts`), the page navigated there and the collection
   opened (`engine.open`: where the route already named it, after a load of it that failed, the
   navigation alone would not). The engine then loads the new collection into the same mount. A
   failure shows under the input, with Retry when trying again may help (see "Data"), which loads
   the same collection again from the same source; its error says whose it is (`from`), and only a
   pasted link's own failure marks the field invalid (`aria-invalid`, its red border), never a
   preset's or the engine's. One load runs at a time: a link or a preset chosen meanwhile is
   dropped, and Load says "Loading…". A load that lands, or fails, once the route has moved on
   (Back meanwhile, the host leaving the project) or once the App has gone saves, navigates and
   says nothing, so it never undoes the visitor's move; `App` stops the loader as it unmounts and
   starts it again (StrictMode mounts twice).

**Restore on `/`**: an App that mounts on `/` navigates to the saved collection (`restoreTarget`),
never to the default playlist, and it looks only when it mounts, so Back to `/` (a route update,
not a remount) stays there. A fresh mount (the host opening the project again) restores again.
Opening someone else's shared link does not replace the visitor's saved one, since only a pasted
link or a pressed preset is saved. Storage that is blocked or full just forgets (`safeStorage`).

## Popular on Spotify

Under the link form, `src/shell/Presets.tsx` lists the twelve playlists of `src/presets.ts`
(`PRESETS`, three-free): Spotify's own, each opened through the proxy when the list was made
(2026-10-31), the page's default first. Each is a `Preset`: its `ref`, Spotify's `name`, a `tag`
(a genre or a mood; a track count would drift) and its `cover`, the embed page's `coverArt` (300 px
JPEGs of 21 to 40 KB on `i.scdn.co`, and the chart's on `charts-images.scdn.co`), hotlinked as the
page already does for track art and never stored here. Spotify renews some editorial covers; an
old URL keeps answering, and a new one is an edit of `presets.ts`. A playlist Spotify retires
answers 404, with the message a pasted link gets: replace it there.

A press loads the playlist as a pasted link loads (the link loader, see "Personalisation"), so it
is saved as the visitor's last and the URL follows. The preset of the collection **on show** is the
current one (`aria-current="true"`, the accent's tint and border, as a switch that is on), and
pressing it does nothing. On show, not in the URL: after a failed first load nothing is current,
so pressing the default loads and opens it, and after a failed change of collection the one still
shown stays current, while pressing the failed one asks for it again (`engine.open` asks again
for a ref whose own collection is not the one on show). The one being loaded says "Loading…" in
place of its tag and is `aria-busy`, its cover dimmed (a phone's chip hides its tag). Each button
is named by its playlist and its tag ("Top 50 - Global Global chart"), the cover `alt=""`.

Its looks follow the layout (see "The look"): rows 54 px high in the rail (a 44 px cover), scrolling
in what it leaves them; a row of cards 184 px wide with 48 px covers in the tablet header,
scrolling sideways with a thin scrollbar and fading out at its right edge, its end padding the
fade's 48 px; chips 44 px high with 36 px covers on a phone, the tag clipped out of sight but kept
in each name, inside its chip (`.cdp-preset` is positioned, so no tag reaches past the page's edge).
The covers are `loading="lazy"` and `decoding="async"`, sized by their attributes (48 × 48) and the
CSS, with no `fetchpriority`. Chrome fetches a lazy image within a long distance of the viewport,
so on a phone and in the rail all twelve come with the page (about 340 KB); only the tablet
header's sideways row leaves those past its end until it scrolls near.

```
                      build_clear_materials(look)), build_clear.py (its web scene: the same names
                      and the door's), export.py (the background entry: CDP_MODEL picks the
                      build), hero.py (the Cycles stills: the suitcase's hero and lid views, the
                      Clear's hero and inside)
art/blender/tools/    bl.py: a client for a running Blender's add-on socket (design sessions only)
art/pipeline/         build.mjs: Blender headless, then gltf-transform (given clear, the Clear's)
art/.out/             scratch: the raw exports, the renders and one-off scripts (gitignored)
```

`art/README.md` lists the scripts. `art:build` (`art/pipeline/build.mjs`) and `art:build:clear`
(the same script given `clear`, which it passes on to Blender as `CDP_MODEL=clear`) need **Blender
5.2.2 LTS** (`/Applications/Blender.app`, or `BLENDER_BIN`) and run it with `--background
--factory-startup --python-exit-code 1` (Blender exits 0 when a script raises) on `export.py`,
which execs `cdkit.py`, `parts.py` and `build_player.py` for the suitcase, or `cdkit.py`,
`parts.py`, `clear.py` and `build_clear.py` for the Clear (any other `CDP_MODEL` stops it), sets
**`WEB = True`** (cdkit's web quality: about half the segments, bevels at a resolution of 2 at
most, none of the Cycles-only shader tricks: the leather grain, the lining's quilt and the cloth's
weave are normal maps the page makes at runtime), turns curves and text into meshes, gives the
tiled materials (`leather`, `trim`, `lining`, `fabric`, `strap`, `deck`, `grille`) box-mapped UVs
at one unit per 5 cm and the printed ones (`art_disc`, `art_booklet`, `card_print`, `tag_print`,
`lcd_screen`) planar UVs over their bounds, and writes `art/.out/cd-player.raw.glb` or
`art/.out/cd-player-clear.raw.glb`. gltf-transform then dedups accessors, meshes and textures
(never materials: the prints start identical and would merge into one), welds, prunes (keeping
the empty pivots, and the UVs that no texture in the file uses yet) and meshopt-compresses it into
`public/assets/cd-player.glb` or `public/assets/cd-player-clear.glb`, both **committed** (about
100,633 triangles and 777 KB for the suitcase, 106,246 and 802 KB for the Clear, at the time of
writing). **The budget** (`model-contract.test.ts`, for each): 4 MB, 150k triangles,
`EXT_meshopt_compression`, and meshes only on leaf nodes, so quantisation never scales a pivot.
Rebuild a model after any change to its scripts (`cdkit.py` and `parts.py` are both models'). The
lettering (the suitcase's deck, the Clear's logos, silkscreen and keys) uses macOS fonts (Avenir
Next, DIN Alternate and Futura, loaded by `use_scene` in `cdkit.py`); elsewhere Blender's own font
stands in and the GLB changes.

**What the scripts parent where.** In the suitcase the deck's printed marks are the deck's
children, never a key's or the knob's; the tonearm's moving parts (yoke, pin, tube, counterweight,
headshell, cartridge, lift, stylus) hang from `arm_pivot` while its base, collar, ring, rest,
cueing lever and anti-skate dial stay on the deck; the five rack slots are empties under
`lid_hinge`, posed by `rack_pose(i)` in `parts.py` (slot 0 lowest and leaning out most, each slot
up 20 mm higher, 1.6 mm closer to the lining and 1.5° straighter; `RACK_X`, `RACK_PITCH`; the
cascade's frame has its cheeks `RACK_CHEEK` either side of `RACK_X`); the VU segments share one
material `vu`; the handle sits on the tub's front between the clasps and the tag hangs from its
left D-ring over the grille. `model-contract.test.ts` holds the parenting. The lid's layout is the
cascade (`RACK_LAYOUT` in `parts.py`); the grid without the crystal case is the other branch of
the same code.

**The Clear model** (`clear.py`, in `parts.py`'s axes and millimetres, with its disc and case).
The shell is a front plate (the window's 128 mm opening cut in it, the speakers' perforated
grilles set in it), a back plate and one hollow band between them for the sides, the top and the
bottom, an outline round an inner outline: never a filled block, which would refract every part
inside it. Inside, the board runs along the bottom (its chips, the LCD, the VU strip and the
silkscreened logo), a speaker stands either side of the window, the motor and the laser's arm
stand behind the disc, and four AA cells lie across the back under the top. The door's bezel,
glass, pull and knuckle hang from its hinge (`door_hinge` once built), left of the window, whose

`build_clear.py` gives what the page drives the contract's names (`PIVOTS`) and adds `disc_seat`
and `case_seat` where the disc and the case stand. The web scene is at rest: the door shut, the
arm off the disc's rim, the rack's slots empty, the prints plain planes for the page to paint; a
render plays (the arm over the disc, discs in the rack, the LCD and seven VU segments lit, the
plate and the tag lettered).

**Materials, skins and looks.** `build_materials(palette)` makes every suitcase material; the
seven the skins recolour (`SKIN_ROLES` in `parts.py`: `leather`, `trim`, `thread`, `lining`,
`hardware`, `deck`, `grille`) take their colours from a palette of sRGB hex values, Blush
(`BLUSH`) by default, so a render of another skin passes its palette and nothing else changes.
`hardware` is every metal fitting (the list is under "Looks"). `build_clear_materials(look)` makes
the Clear's on top of `build_materials()` (the disc's and the case's): the four a look colours,
from the swatches `clear_look(spec)` reads out of `src/clear-looks.json` (a part or a field the
file lacks stops the run, naming what is wrong), and the fixed ones of the parts inside, the
silkscreen white, and dark on the white board.

`hero.py` renders the Cycles stills headless (Metal through `enable_gpu` in `cdkit.py`; the first
run compiles the kernels for about two minutes; `render()` renders by scene name under
`--background`):

    CDP_MODEL=suitcase|clear CDP_HERO_VIEW=hero|lid|inside CDP_HERO_QUALITY=preview|final \
      /Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --factory-startup \
      --python-exit-code 1 --python apps/cd-player/art/blender/scripts/hero.py

`CDP_MODEL` is `suitcase` unless set. The suitcase's `hero` is the three-quarter product still the
design was approved from, `lid` looks straight into the open lid; the Clear's `hero` is the
three-quarter still between its stand and its case, `inside` a close-up of the board, the LCD and
the mechanism through the shell. `preview` is 800 × 600 at 24 samples, `final` 1600 × 1200 at 160.

before anything is built, naming the valid ones; so does an unknown `CDP_SKIN`, a `CDP_CLEAR_LOOK`

prints is the gallery's thumbnails (`apps/portfolio/public/thumbnails`), so anyone can rerun it;
the PNG lands in `art/.out/v2-<view>-<quality>.png`, or in `art/.out/skins/<id>/` when
`CDP_SKIN=<id>` (an id of `src/skins.json`: see "Looks") renders that colourway instead of Blush;
the Clear's, in its default look or the one `CDP_CLEAR_LOOK=<shell>,<frame>,<board>,<light>`
names, in `art/.out/clear/<shell>-<frame>-<board>-<light>/clear-<view>-<quality>.png`. The Clear's
studio lets the camera see the flat backdrop through the shell (`see_through_backdrop`), which
would otherwise show the HDRI behind it as if its back were smoked, and sets dark flags round it
that only reflections see (`clear_flags`), so its edges and walls read against the light backdrop.
They are for the owner's gates and the docs; the build never runs them, and no render is in the
repository.

**The socket tool.** `art/blender/tools/bl.py` drives a running Blender through the Blender Lab MCP
add-on's socket at `127.0.0.1:9876`: it sends `{"type": "execute", "code": …, "strict_json": false}`
followed by a NUL byte and reads JSON up to the next NUL; the code assigns a JSON-serialisable dict
to `result` to return data (`python3 -I bl.py <file.py>`, or `-c "<code>"`). It is for design
sessions in a live Blender only: nothing in the build needs it. In a live Blender a render goes
through the window's scene under `temp_override` (`render()` in `cdkit.py`), and the first Cycles
render on Metal spends about two minutes compiling its kernels.

## First load

Nothing on the first screen is a picture of the scene, so a change to the model, the lights, the
framing or the prints never needs a new one. The server renders the page's own backdrop (the
look's pastel with the first track's art blurred into it) and, in the scene's place, the **loader**
(`src/shell/Loader.tsx`, `cdp-loader`): a 44 px disc glyph (inline SVG) spun by CSS from the first
paint, and one line in the UI face, "Loading the player". It is plain from the first paint, with no
entrance (text that enters at opacity 0 counts for LCP only at its next repaint), and stands where
"Open the player" will (`.cdp-gate`'s place, the middle of the stage). Under
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` its disc stands still. The presets' covers are the only pictures
the server renders, outside the stage (see "Popular on Spotify").

**The count.** While the scene boots the line gives the download of the chosen model's GLB,
"Loading the player 63%" (`loaderPercent` and `loaderLine` in `src/view.ts`: a whole percent, held
between 0 and 100). `loadPlayerModel(profile, url, onProgress, signal)` in `src/scene/model.ts`
fetches the GLB itself (`downloadModel`, then GLTFLoader's `parseAsync`) and reports its request
going out (with no fraction), each chunk, where `downloadFraction` makes a fraction only when the
response names its length (`Content-Length`) and holds it at 0.99 at most (a compressed transfer's
decoded bytes can outrun the length long before the end), and the end of the stream, the only
report of 1. The boot's reports go to `PlayerSceneProps.onProgress` (`PlayerScene` drops a
cancelled load's; a later change of model reports to the picker, see "Changing models"), then
`App`, which keeps the percent and sets it only when it changes, so the download's chunks
re-render the page once per percent at most. `PlayerScene` aborts the download of a load it lets
go of (a failed boot, StrictMode's first run, a remount, another model chosen), so "Try again"
after a stall asks for the GLB anew. The
model fetches it itself because three's FileLoader joins a load to a request for the same URL
still in flight, a stalled one included. The scene chunk's own download is not measured: until the
GLB's first chunk, and while its size is unknown, the line has no number (a GLB served with no
length shows 100 % at its end alone). "Try again" clears the count, so a boot starts from none,
even when a chunk of the failed boot's download came after the failure, before its scene let go.

**Hand-overs.** The loader gives way to "Open the player" once the page has settled without
starting the scene (a software rasteriser, reduced motion: `manual` in `App`, since the boot's
events stay as they were), and to the failure when a boot fails; "Open the player" brings it back
for the boot. A boot that stalls fails too, so no spinner runs forever (as long as the stage has a
size, which the layout keeps: see "The look"): **the stall watch**
(`watchStall` in `src/boot.ts`, which `App` runs while the phase is `booting`) is armed as the boot
starts, armed again by every progress event of the GLB (with a fraction or without one), and over
once the download is complete or the phase leaves `booting`. Complete is the 1 of the stream's
end, never a chunk's count, so a compressed GLB counted past its length, or one served with no
length, is watched to its last byte. 20 s with no event (`BOOT_STALL_MS`) fails the boot with
`BootStallError` (see "Failures"), whose message names the part it waited on: the scene's code
until the GLB's request goes out (its first event), the GLB's download after. It times silence,
not the boot: the shaders compile after the download without an event (seconds on SwiftShader),
and a total timeout would fail a slow machine. The scene's code sends no event as it comes, so it
alone must come whole within those 20 s: on a link of about 100 kbit/s or less it cannot, and the
boot fails as a stall while its bytes still come. "Try again" then waits on the same request (a
browser keeps one fetch per module URL while it is on its way), so the code still comes; a request
that hangs for good stalls again, and only a reload asks anew. On `ready` the canvas fades in over
600 ms and the loader fades out with it (`.cdp-scene.is-live ~ .cdp-loader`: the loader comes
after the scene layer in the DOM, so it also draws over the invisible canvas, with
`pointer-events: none`), then unmounts.

the player" are single short lines, far smaller than the note: neither can take the LCP from it,
not even "Open the player", which appears only once the page has settled. On a machine with a GPU
Chrome opens the scene by itself during the run, and the scene's boot costs desktop a few points of
Performance (blocking time). To measure what the runner measures there, add `--disable-gpu` to the
`chromeFlags` of a copy of `lighthouserc.cjs`. A Spotify failure during a run (the proxy's 502 for
a prefetch, or an album-art image its CDN fails to serve with its CORS header, which the scene
loads with `crossOrigin = 'anonymous'`) logs a console error, which costs that run Best Practices
points.

## Deploying

The Vercel project **`ncam-cd-player`** (Root Directory `apps/cd-player`, the Vite preset,
`SITE_URL` set to the host's public origin) also deploys `api/spotify.ts` as the Function at
`/api/spotify`. `vercel.json` sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on every path except `/api/`
(the handler sends its own), revalidates `/assets/cd-player.glb` and `/assets/cd-player-clear.glb`
(`public, max-age=0, must-revalidate`: their names never change), rewrites `/playlist/<id>` and
`/album/<id>` to `index.html` for the standalone page, and keeps Dependabot's branches from
deploying. Docker: the compose service `cd-player` shares the host container's network
(`network_mode: service:portfolio`, 9011 published on `portfolio`), the `Dockerfile` takes
`CD_PLAYER_REMOTE_URL` as an `ARG`, and nginx has the `cd_player` upstream and a
`cd-player.localhost` server block.

**Owner tasks before the merge goes live** (`docs/deployment.md` → "Adding a remote to the live
site"), in this order:

1. Create the Vercel project `ncam-cd-player` (Root Directory `apps/cd-player`, `SITE_URL` set)
   and deploy it.
2. Check that the Function answers:
   `https://ncam-cd-player.vercel.app/api/spotify?type=playlist&id=37i9dQZEVXbMDoHDwVN2tF` should
   return the playlist as JSON (the Function's build cannot be tried locally).
3. On the host project, set
   `CD_PLAYER_REMOTE_URL=https://ncam-cd-player.vercel.app/remoteEntry.js` (**with**
   `/remoteEntry.js`) and redeploy the host.
4. Merge. Merged first, the host bakes in `localhost:9011` and the gallery lists a page that cannot
   load.

## Known facts and gotchas

- **Spotify answers an unknown id with HTTP 200**: a page whose `pageProps` is `{ status: 404, … }`
  with no `state`, so `readEntity` checks for the entity itself (no entity on the 404 page is a 404;
  no entity on any other page is a 502).
- **The embed reports itself paused right after `loadUri`**, until the audio starts. The engine
  ignores paused updates until `playback_started` (`awaitingStart`), or every new track would read
  as paused at once.
- **Logged out, and in Safari** (which blocks the embed's third-party cookies by default), the embed
  plays Spotify's **30 s previews**; full tracks need a Spotify login in the same browser. Headless
  Chromium gets the previews too.
- **Cycles blocks shadow rays at glass**, so in a Cycles render whatever sits inside the crystal
  case comes out dark; `mat()` in `cdkit.py` lets shadow rays through a Transparent BSDF, only when
  `WEB` is false (the Clear's shell tints them as it is tinted, `_shadow_colour` in `clear.py`). It
  is a design-render concern only: the web scene's glass casts no shadow at all
  (`upgradeMaterials` in `src/scene/materials.ts`).
- **three's transmission samples only opaque surfaces** (the pass it samples draws the opaque
  objects alone, every clear material here being front-sided), so a clear part seen through
  another (the Clear's stand through its shell's band, at the orbit's end) lacks its own tint and
  refraction there: only its opaque parts show. The page reads right as it is, since what each
  clear part frames (the booklet in the case, the discs on the stand, the board and the speakers in
  the shell) is opaque.
- On `/projects/cd-player/playlist/<id>` the host titles the page with the remote's `head`
  (`<name> · CD Player · ncam.dev`) and its description; on `/projects/cd-player` it uses the
  registry's name and tagline. The visitors' pages are not in the sitemap (`routeAware: true`, no
  `sitemapPaths`).
- **The embed ignores a seek to under a second** (`seek(0)` included), and a paused embed reports a
  seek only once it plays again; Previous and Stop rewind through `restart()` and a seek to 1 s
  (see "Playback").
- **Spotify's iFrame API script** (`SpotifyEmbedController`) holds commands while its iframe loads
  and sends them on the iframe's READY, so a command to a player still loading is not lost. Its
  `loadUri` never skips the uri it already holds (`currentUri` is never set): every call loads the
  embed's page again. Once a track has ended, the embed has dropped its list, and a command sent to
  it throws "Cannot perform operation; no list was loaded." inside its iframe; only a `loadUri`
  brings it back (hence the end watchdog and the first load's `play()`, in "Playback").
- **The disc's printed layers are microns apart** (`make_disc` in `parts.py`: the label 20 µm over
  the clear body's top, the foil rings 5 µm over the label), as a Cycles render wants them; a depth
  buffer tells them apart only with the near plane well out. At 0.02 m its step at the player was
  about 4 µm, and every disc's rim and hub ring came out ragged, toothed like a gear, worst in the
  rack. A new layer as thin, or a frame that brings the camera nearer, needs the same check
  (`framing.test.ts`).
- **The Clear's shell shows a faint dotted line at 1440 × 900** along its lower edge and its
  corner curves: sub-pixel aliasing of its 0.5 mm bevel's highlight, not z-fighting (the band's
  ends share the plates' inner faces, and parting them by 0.1 mm changed nothing). It is smooth on
  a larger stage; a wider bevel or another material would take it away, and the owner approved
  the shell as it is.
- **The Clear costs about a quarter more per frame** than the suitcase: 177 against 223 fps
  uncapped at 1440 × 900 in headless Chromium on an M4 Pro (1,739 draw calls a frame against 772,
  and the transmission pass); both hold 60 fps under vsync there. A real phone's GPU is the
  owner's check.
- **The arm's lift is the suitcase's**: the director lifts the arm 6 mm along its pivot parent's Y
  (`ARM_LIFT_M` in `rig.ts`), off the suitcase's disc; the Clear's disc stands upright, so there
  the lift moves the laser's arm parallel to the disc, behind it, which shows nothing amiss.
- **In Chrome a backdrop filter bounds its descendants' backdrop filters**: an element inside a
  panel that had one blurred nothing behind it of the panel's own content (the presets ghosted
  through the picker's popover). Hence the panels' glass on their `::before`; keep any new frosted
  child's ancestors free of `backdrop-filter`, `filter`, `opacity` below 1 and the like.
- **iOS Safari zooms into a focused field set under 16 px**, and this full-screen page would stay
  zoomed: the link input is 16 px in the phone layout and the sideways one (14 px elsewhere).
- The suitcase's Stop, Repeat, MODE (Shuffle) and knob are small targets: on a 390 px phone a key
  is about 9 px across and the knob 18, and from the hero view the knob hides much of the MODE
  key. The now-playing card therefore has a DOM control for each (Stop, Repeat, Shuffle and the
  Seek slider, besides Previous, Play and Next), and the keyboard keeps Space, N and P. The
  tracklist does not reorder under Shuffle: it lists the playlist, and only the play order
  (`seq.order`) changes.
- Console noise that is not this app's: `THREE.Clock: This module has been deprecated` comes from
  R3F 9.8.2 itself, and `Allow attribute will take precedence over 'allowfullscreen'` from
  Spotify's iframe.
- Rebuilding after a build that overrode `CD_PLAYER_REMOTE_URL` (the Lighthouse build with
  `LH_PORT_OFFSET`, a host smoke against :9111) needs `pnpm build --force`: a Turbo cache hit
  writes the cached outputs back but leaves the overridden build's extra chunks in
  `apps/portfolio/.output`.
- `curl` and `wget` are blocked in the agents' environment: probe `/api/spotify` with a small
  `.mjs` that calls `fetch`, run with `node`.
